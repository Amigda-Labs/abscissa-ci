# Tile Installation Setup

Use this skill when a task asks what is needed to set up, prepare for, or plan a tile installation.

This skill does not compute area or tile counts. Use `tile-area-computation` and `tile-counting` when the user needs quantities.

## Core Rules

- Treat every checklist as a draft for contractor or qualified installer review.
- Separate confirmed project facts from assumptions and missing inputs.
- Do not invent tile quantities, mortar quantities, rates, cure times, waterproofing details, or movement joint locations.
- Use product-specific manufacturer data sheets when a brand/system is specified.
- Flag wet areas, exterior areas, submerged areas, stone, large-format tile, wood subfloors, gypsum underlayments, and existing tile as higher-review conditions.
- Keep output contractor-friendly and practical, not academic.

## Inputs To Capture

Capture these from the user or source materials before producing a setup checklist:

- Location: floor, wall, shower/wet area, exterior, balcony/deck, pool/submerged, countertop, backsplash.
- Substrate: concrete slab, screed, cement backer board, plywood system, existing tile, gypsum, masonry, drywall, or unknown.
- Tile: ceramic, porcelain, natural stone, glass, cement, mosaic, large-format/slab, rectified/non-rectified, size, thickness, finish, exterior rating if relevant.
- Exposure: dry interior, wet interior, direct sunlight, freeze-thaw, heavy traffic, chemical exposure, heat/steam, underfloor heating.
- Layout constraints: room dimensions, openings, drains, slopes, perimeter conditions, movement joints, tile pattern, grout joint width, transitions to adjacent finishes.
- Spec inputs: drawings, manufacturer instructions, TCNA/ANSI method if specified, waterproofing/crack-isolation/uncoupling system, required grout/sealant type.

If critical inputs are missing, proceed with a draft only and list the missing inputs clearly.

## Materials Checklist

Group materials as `Required`, `Conditional`, or `Verify`.

Core materials:

- Tile, including extra allowance if quantity is already calculated by another skill.
- Setting mortar or adhesive selected for the tile, substrate, and exposure.
- Grout selected for joint width, tile sensitivity, traffic, and exposure.
- Movement joint sealant, backer rod, profiles, or prefabricated movement-joint trims where needed.
- Tile spacers or leveling clips/wedges where appropriate.
- Clean water, mixing buckets, sponges, cloths, and cleanup materials.

Substrate preparation:

- Patching/repair mortar, skim coat, self-leveling underlayment, screed, or leveling compound when the substrate is not flat/sound.
- Primer when required by the patching, self-leveling, membrane, gypsum, or manufacturer system.
- Cement backer board, fasteners, alkali-resistant mesh tape, and board seam mortar when backer board is part of the assembly.
- Crack-isolation, uncoupling, or anti-fracture membrane when specified or when slab/substrate movement risk is present.

Wet, exterior, and specialty conditions:

- Waterproofing membrane or shower/tanking system for wet areas where required by the assembly.
- Drainage/slope components for exterior work, balconies, decks, showers, and wet rooms.
- Exterior-rated tile, exterior-rated mortar, and exterior-suitable grout/sealant for exterior work.
- Sealers for natural stone, cement tile, porous tile, or grout when required by the product.
- Edge trims, transition profiles, stair nosings, thresholds, and expansion profiles where the finish terminates or changes plane.

## Tools Checklist

Group tools as `Absolute Must`, `Strongly Recommended`, or `Conditional`.

`Absolute Must` means the installer cannot realistically complete a normal tile
setup without it. Do not put convenience tools in this group. For example, a
mixing drill is useful on larger jobs but is not an absolute must for small
batches that can be mixed by hand according to the product instructions.

Absolute must for most tile setup:

- Tape measure, pencil/marker, straightedge or level, and square.
- Clean buckets or mixing containers.
- Margin trowel or hand mixing tool.
- Notched trowel sized for the selected tile and mortar/adhesive.
- Tile spacers or another method to maintain consistent grout joints.
- Tile cutter, wet saw, or approved cutting method when any cut tiles are required.
- Rubber grout float.
- Grout sponge, wash bucket, clean water, and cloths.
- Eye protection, gloves, and knee protection.

Strongly recommended for cleaner, faster, or more consistent work:

- Chalk line or laser level for layout.
- Flat trowel for keying-in mortar and back-buttering where needed.
- Rubber mallet or beating block.
- Tile leveling clips/wedges, especially for larger-format tile.
- Rubbing stone or polishing pad for cut edges.
- HEPA vacuum or wet cleanup method for dust control.
- Hearing protection when using powered cutting tools.

Conditional or project-specific:

- Mixing drill and correct paddle for larger batches, stiffer mortars, or professional production work.
- Wet saw with diamond blade for porcelain, stone, dense tile, or precise/visible cuts.
- Tile nippers, hole saws, or diamond drill bits for curves, pipes, drains, outlets, or penetrations.
- Scraper, chisel, grinder, surface prep tool, patching tools, or self-leveling tools when substrate correction is required.
- Caulk/sealant gun and tooling for movement joints or changes in plane.
- Suction cups or slab handling tools for large-format or heavy tile.
- Respiratory protection when dust cannot be controlled by wet cutting or HEPA methods.

Use the detailed category list below to decide why each item is needed.

Layout and measurement:

- Tape measure, pencil/marker, chalk line, square, straightedge, level or laser level.
- Spacers, story pole or layout stick, and dry-layout area for checking cuts and pattern.

Substrate and prep:

- Scraper, chisel, grinder or surface prep tool, HEPA vacuum or wet cleanup method, broom, and dust control.
- Buckets, measuring container, margin trowel, and optional mixing drill with correct paddle.
- Screed tools, patching tools, or self-leveling tools when substrate correction is required.

Setting tile:

- Notched trowels sized for the tile and mortar system.
- Flat trowel/margin trowel for keying-in mortar and back-buttering where needed.
- Rubber mallet or beating block, tile leveling system, spacers, straightedge.
- Suction cups or slab handling tools for large-format or heavy tile.

Cutting and drilling:

- Manual tile cutter for straight cuts where suitable.
- Wet saw with diamond blade for porcelain, stone, dense tile, or precise cuts.
- Tile nippers, hole saws or diamond drill bits, rubbing stone or polishing pad for cut edges.

Grouting and cleanup:

- Rubber grout float, grout sponge, wash buckets, microfiber cloths, haze remover if manufacturer-approved.
- Caulk/sealant gun and tooling for movement joints or changes in plane.

Safety/PPE:

- Eye protection, cut-resistant gloves, hearing protection, knee pads, and suitable respiratory protection when dust cannot be controlled.
- Use wet cutting, integrated water delivery, or HEPA dust control for silica-containing tile, stone, mortar, or substrate cutting.

## Setup Workflow

1. State the scope and confirmed facts.
2. Check the substrate: sound, clean, dry where required, flat, rigid, properly sloped if wet/exterior, and compatible with the selected assembly.
3. Identify review-critical conditions: movement joints, cracks, deflection, moisture, exterior exposure, wet area waterproofing, drains/slopes, and product compatibility.
4. Select materials by assembly, not by habit: tile type, substrate, exposure, and manufacturer system must align.
5. Plan layout: centerlines or control lines, dry-lay, avoid narrow perimeter cuts where practical, confirm grout joint width, transitions, and expansion/movement accommodation.
6. Plan cutting and dust control before installation starts.
7. Produce the materials checklist grouped by `Required`, `Conditional`, and `Verify`.
8. Produce the tools checklist grouped by `Absolute Must`, `Strongly Recommended`, and `Conditional`.
9. List assumptions, missing inputs, risks, and what a human reviewer must confirm.

## Technical Defaults

- Substrate should be clean, sound, flat, and free of bond-breaking materials.
- Industry guidance commonly requires at least 80% mortar coverage in dry areas and 95% in wet/exterior areas; natural stone typically needs 95% coverage.
- Do not use spot bonding as a normal tile-setting method.
- Porcelain and dense tile commonly need high-bond or polymer/latex-fortified mortar unless a proprietary system specifies otherwise.
- Organic adhesive/mastic should not be assumed suitable for floors, wet/high-performance areas, membranes, exterior work, or large-format tile.
- Movement joints must be planned at perimeters, changes in plane, and where the design/standard/manufacturer requires them. Existing structural movement joints must be honored through the tilework.
- Exterior tile work requires exterior-rated tile and setting materials, drainage/slope planning, and movement accommodation.
- Manufacturer pot life, open time, grout wait time, traffic time, and water exposure time must be verified from the product data sheet.
